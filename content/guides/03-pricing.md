A list price is the last number, not the first. Write what you would accept, what a similar sale included, and what the costs take out. Then publish. The short notes are [How to price my house to sell](/tips/price-the-property-you-have), [Home pricing strategy](/tips/write-three-numbers-before-you-list), and [Write your bottom line before the first showing](/tips/write-your-bottom-line-early). This guide is the order those notes belong in.

## Three numbers, on paper

Write three figures before you pick a list price. The first is the bottom line: the check you need, or the lowest price you will sign, after you have looked at your loan payoff and your move. The second is a sale you can describe: a nearby house that sold, what it included, and how its condition differed from yours. [How to read comps](/tips/match-what-was-included-in-a-sale) is the short version of that comparison. The third is the list price you will publish, which has to leave room for a buyer to negotiate. [List price negotiation room](/tips/leave-room-to-negotiate) covers that gap. If the list price and the bottom line are the same number, you have no room, and you should know that before the first offer.

Keep the notes. [Price reduction strategy](/tips/keep-price-notes-in-writing) is about writing why you chose the number, so a later cut is a decision and not a mood. A price you cannot explain in one paragraph is a price you will struggle to defend on a Tuesday night.

## Costs and credits before the sign goes up

Seller costs come out before you are paid. Commission is negotiated. It is not a rate this site can fill in. Use the [commission comparison](/tools/commission) with the rates you were quoted, and the [net proceeds calculator](/tools/net-proceeds) with the payoff, fees, credits, and repairs you actually expect. [Seller credits closing costs](/tips/list-credits-before-you-pick-a-price) says to list credits before you fall in love with a headline price. A credit is a price cut with extra steps. Write it as dollars.

If the sale may produce a gain, read [IRS Publication 523](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p523) before you treat the whole check as spendable. The tax guide later on this site walks the vocabulary. Pricing day only needs the warning: the wire at closing is not the same as the after-tax number, and the publication is the federal starting point.

## After the first offers

Every offer changes the net. [Net proceeds after repairs](/tips/rerun-the-net-after-each-bid) is the habit: run the sheet again when a buyer asks for a repair or a credit. Do not compare two offers by the first line of price if one closes in two weeks and the other asks for a new roof. The offers guide on this site is the place for deadlines and counters. Pricing's job is to keep the dollar comparison honest.

If nothing arrives, return to the written notes. A reduction is a new list price with a reason you already know how to write. It is not a secret. [How to price my house to sell](/tips/price-the-property-you-have) still applies: price the property you have this week, not the one you remember from a spring five years ago.

## Checklist

- Write the bottom line in dollars, after a current loan payoff quote.
- Write one comparable sale and what was included: roof, appliances, repairs, credits.
- Write the list price last, and the gap between it and the bottom line.
- Type payoff, quoted commissions, closing costs, credits, and repairs into the net proceeds calculator.
- List any seller credit as dollars before you advertise a price.
- If a gain is possible, read IRS Publication 523 before you spend the wire in your head.
- Save the pricing notes in the same folder as the offers.
- Rerun the net after each bid, repair request, or credit.
- If you cut the price, write the reason next to the old number.
- Do not publish a price you have not subtracted costs from.

## Worked examples

### A bottom line and a list price

Suppose you want $40,000 in cash after the sale, and a payoff quote for the mortgage is $210,000 through the date you hope to close. A title company quote for the owner's policy and settlement fees is $2,200. You were quoted listing-side compensation of $12,000 and you plan to offer $8,000 to a buyer's agent. You expect about $1,500 in other closing costs and a $3,000 repair credit. Add those outs: 210,000 + 2,200 + 12,000 + 8,000 + 1,500 + 3,000 = 236,700. Add the cash you want: 276,700. That is a price that produces the bottom line only if every quote holds. If you list at $285,000, the gap is about $8,300 before any further credit. These figures are an example you replace with your quotes. They are not a typical price and not a tax estimate.

Type the same lines into the calculator so a later offer of $280,000 with an extra $4,000 credit is a new net, not a feeling.

### Two sales that are not the same house

Suppose one house on your street sold at $300,000 and included a new roof and a closing credit of $5,000. Yours needs the roof conversation and you do not want to offer that credit. Matching the $300,000 headline copies a sale that included different work. Write the roof bid, subtract it, and write the credit you will not offer. [How to read comps](/tips/match-what-was-included-in-a-sale) is the short form of this comparison. The number you publish should survive that paragraph.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the list price because it matches a mortgage payoff plus a round profit, with no costs taken out.
- Comparing a renovated sale to a house you are selling as-is.
- Ignoring a seller credit until the offer arrives.
- Treating a payoff on last month's statement as the payoff on closing day.
- Cutting the price without writing why the first number failed.
- Spending the gross price in your head and calling it proceeds.

## When to call a pro

Hire an appraiser when you and a buyer are far apart and you want an opinion of value you did not write yourself. Ask what the fee is before the visit. A typical range for a residential appraisal is often several hundred dollars. Your invoice is the number that counts. Hire an agent or a pricing analyst if you want them to pull recent sales and walk the house. Their pay is the agreement you sign, not a figure this page can set.

Call your loan servicer for a payoff quote good through a specific date. Call a tax advisor if the sale is a second home, a rental, or a house you inherited. Publication 523 is the reading. It is not a return. Call a title company for the fee quote you will actually pay in your county. A national range is a poor substitute for that one-page estimate.

## Questions

### Is the online estimate the price?

Use it as a prompt, not a conclusion. It does not know the roof, the credit you will offer, or the payoff on your loan. Walk the comparable sale and write what was included. Then use your own sheet.

### How much room should I leave for negotiation?

Enough that a reasonable offer can land at or above your bottom line. If you have no gap, say so in your notes and be ready to decline. There is no required percent. [List price negotiation room](/tips/leave-room-to-negotiate) is the short note. The gap is in dollars on your page.

### Should I price low to start a rush?

Only if you have a plan for the offers that follow and a bottom line you will keep. A low price without a way to compare nets is a discount, not a strategy. Write the bottom line first. If the low price is already under it, do not publish it.

### Do pricing notes go to the buyer?

The bottom line does not. The list price does. Keep the sheet in your file so you can answer your own questions at offer time. The buyer sees the contract, the disclosure, and the credits you agree to in writing.
