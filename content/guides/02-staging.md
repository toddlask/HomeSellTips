Staging is the order a stranger walks, not a pile of new furniture. Showings are the hours you will actually open the door. Do the path first, then the rooms, then the clock. The short notes stay separate: [Declutter before selling house](/tips/clear-surfaces-before-you-decorate), [Depersonalize house for sale](/tips/pack-personal-items-early), and [FSBO showings](/tips/plan-showing-hours-before-you-advertise).

## The path a visitor uses

Stand at the curb and walk to the door the way a guest will. The entry is the first room, even when it is only a mat and a light. [Entryway staging](/tips/finish-the-entry-first) is the short version. Then walk the path through each room without stepping sideways around a chair. [Furniture arrangement for staging](/tips/leave-a-clear-path-through-each-room) covers that path. If you have to turn your shoulders, the room is not ready for a photo or a showing.

Open window coverings the same way in every room you show, so the light is a choice and not a surprise. [Natural light staging tips](/tips/open-the-window-coverings-the-same-way) is that check. Put pet beds, bowls, and litter out of the path before the bell rings. [Selling a house with pets](/tips/put-pet-gear-away-before-showings) is the short note. The animal can live in the house. The gear should not be the first thing in the frame.

## Rooms, closets, and one table

Clear surfaces before you add anything. A bowl, a lamp, and a book are enough on a table that was buried. [Dining table staging ideas](/tips/stop-after-one-simple-table-setting) says to stop after one simple setting. More place settings do not explain the room. They hide the table.

Closets should look used and not packed to the ceiling. [Closet staging tips](/tips/leave-closets-partly-empty) is the short note. A buyer opens the closet. If the door barely closes, the photo of the empty bed does not matter. Pack the out-of-season and the personal early, while you still have weeks. Personal photos and mail are the items people forget because they stopped seeing them.

Do this before the photographer, not after. Listing photos lock in whatever the rooms looked like that morning. A showing that is tidier than the photos is a nice surprise. A showing that is messier than the photos is the visit people remember.

## Showing hours that you can keep

Write the hours before you advertise them. A plan that says "any time" becomes a missed call and a dark house. Pick blocks you can keep for the first two weeks, including a way to leave with the pet and the valuables. If you are selling without an agent, [FSBO showings](/tips/plan-showing-hours-before-you-advertise) is the worksheet. If an agent holds the lockbox, you still need to know which hours the house will be empty and who confirms the appointment.

Leave lights on for the path you want used, and leave the rest of the house in the same state as the photos. A last-minute pile in the tub reads as a room you hoped they would skip. They will not skip it.

## Checklist

- Walk from the curb to the back door and move anything that blocks a shoulder.
- Finish the entry before you style a back bedroom.
- Clear surfaces, then add one simple setting, not a second theme.
- Pack personal papers, photos, and medicine out of sight.
- Leave closets partly empty so the door closes and the shelf is visible.
- Set window coverings the same way in each room you show.
- Put pet gear away and plan where the pet will be during a visit.
- Write showing hours you can keep, and a same-day reset that takes minutes.
- Match the showing to the photos. Do not restage the night before and forget the camera already left.
- Turn off water sounds and run the fan you want running before you open the door.

## Worked examples

### Bins, a lamp, and a Saturday block

Suppose three rooms need a path and the entry needs a lamp that works. A typical range for a set of sturdy moving bins, if you buy them rather than borrow them, is about $40 to $150. A typical range for one lamp and a bulb, if the entry has neither, is about $25 to $80. A typical range for a short consult with a stager, if you hire one for a walk-through and a list rather than a full furnish, is about $150 to $500. Full furnishing is a different invoice and can run much higher. Label these as typical ranges. Your receipts are the numbers that go in the budget.

The Saturday block is not a cost. It is a constraint. If you can leave from 11 to 2, write that, and do not also promise a 5 p.m. visit you cannot reset for. One kept block is worth more than four missed ones.

### The closet that ate the photo

Suppose the main closet is full and the bedroom photo is already scheduled. Empty a third of the rod and one shelf the day before, using bins you will need for the move anyway. The short closet note shows the same move. Do not buy a new organizer the week of the shoot. The picture needs space, not hardware. A typical range for a closet system is hundreds of dollars and does not belong in a two-week listing plan unless a door will not close at all.

## Common mistakes

- Decorating a room you have not cleared.
- Setting a table for a crowd in a room that needs a path.
- Promising showings at hours you work or when no one can leave with the pet.
- Leaving mail, medicine, and award plaques in the first sight line.
- Cleaning only the rooms you like and leaving the garage or the utility closet as a surprise.
- Changing the rooms after the photos and then wondering why the visit does not match the ad.

## When to call a pro

Hire a stager when you cannot see the room anymore, or when the house is empty and you want a few pieces so the scale reads. Ask for a written scope: a consult, a partial stage, or a full rental of furniture. Those are different bills. Hire a locksmith if you need a lockbox and you do not already have a way to control the key. Hire a cleaner for the reset only after the packing is done.

You do not need a pro to remove personal items, open the coverings, or write the hours. If you cannot do those three, hiring a photographer will not fix the showing. Do the path first. Then decide whether a paid eye is worth the quote you were given.

## Questions

### Should I buy new furniture to sell the house?

Usually no. Clear, path, and light come first. Buy a lamp or a set of bins if something is missing and the cost is small. Renting a house full of furniture is a quote you compare with the price you hope to gain. This site does not promise that the rental pays for itself.

### What do I do with pets during a visit?

Take them out, or keep them in a planned place with the gear out of the photos. Do not leave a bowl on the only path through the kitchen. Tell the person showing the house about a pet that will be present so nobody is surprised by a door.

### How many showings should I accept in a day?

As many as you can reset for. Two clean visits beat five visits that open onto a sink of dishes. Write the gap you need between them. Twenty minutes is a guess until you time one reset.

### Do buyers expect the house to look like a magazine?

They expect to walk, open a closet, and see the rooms from the photos. A magazine set is optional. A blocked hall is not. Stop when the path is clear and the personal items are packed.
