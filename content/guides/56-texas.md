This is not legal advice. Texas contracts and disclosures are state law plus the facts of your house. The [Texas Real Estate Commission](https://www.trec.texas.gov/) publishes the [Seller's Disclosure Notice](https://www.trec.texas.gov/forms/sellers-disclosure-notice) and explains its [contracts and forms](https://www.trec.texas.gov/agency-information/contracts). Read those pages and ask a Texas real estate lawyer if you are unsure which form fits this sale. The national [Seller closing costs: what sellers pay](/tips/seller-closing-costs) page explains the kinds of costs sellers see anywhere. This guide is the Texas checklist around that page. The short notes cover the disclosure, the cost of selling, seller closing costs, selling without a realtor, and the steps in order.

## The disclosure comes before the brochure

TREC's Seller's Disclosure Notice says it is required for sellers of previously occupied single-family residences and that it contains information required by Section 5.008 of the Texas Property Code about material facts and the physical condition of the property. That is the form's own description. Some sales are outside that requirement. Do not guess that you are one of them because the house "has nothing wrong" or because you are selling it yourself. Read the form's instructions and ask a Texas lawyer if your sale is a new house, a foreclosure, an estate, or anything other than a plain resale you occupied.

Fill the notice from the prep walk, not from memory on the hood of a car. Foundation repairs, prior insurance claims you know about, and systems that do not work belong on the form if it asks. The foundation guide is the check for cracks and movement. Known facts stay known after you paint. A buyer in Texas can still have their own inspection. Your notice is what you knew when you signed it. Update it if you learn something material before closing and your lawyer or agent tells you a correction is required. Do not wait out the buyer's option period hoping they skip the attic.

## What it costs, without a made-up percent

Nobody on this site can tell you the percent of the price a Texas sale "usually" takes. Title insurance in Texas is quoted from the policy you buy, the closing office's fees are on their estimate, and compensation to a broker is whatever you negotiate. Get a written title quote and a written brokerage quote, then use the [net proceeds calculator](/tools/net-proceeds) and the [commission comparison](/tools/commission). Read [Seller closing costs: what sellers pay](/tips/seller-closing-costs) so you recognize payoff, prorations, and credits when they show up on a Texas settlement statement. The statement for your file is still the authority. A national article cannot see your survey, your HOA resale papers, or your payoff.

Ask the title company who pays for the owner's title policy in your deal. Custom is not the contract. Write the choice into the offer you accept. Survey, HOA documents, and a repair credit are separate lines. Add them only when you have a figure.

## Agent or no agent, the steps stay

License holders in Texas are required to use TREC's promulgated contract forms. The forms are public records, so you can read them. If you are not a license holder and you sell without an agent, ask a Texas real estate lawyer which contract to use and to review the offer before you sign. Do not download another state's form because it looked familiar. [Realtor vs FSBO](/tips/list-the-jobs-before-you-choose) is the national job list. The Texas version adds the disclosure notice, the TREC contract conversation, and a title company you pick on purpose.

The order is the same as the rest of this site, with Texas papers inserted. Walk and repair using the prep guide. Complete the disclosure. Price with a payoff quote and a title quote. Photograph the house you disclosed, not a house with the cracks cropped out. Show it. Read offers for the option period, the closing date, and who pays which title policy. Close with the wire rule in the closing guide: call a known number before you send money. Then move on the possession date in the contract.

## Checklist

- Read the TREC Seller's Disclosure Notice and its instructions before you list.
- Ask a Texas lawyer if you are not sure the notice applies to this sale.
- Fill it from a written walk of the house, including prior repairs you know about.
- Order a mortgage payoff quote if a loan remains.
- Get a written title-company estimate and ask who pays for the owner's title policy.
- Get brokerage compensation in writing if you hire a broker, and type it into the commission tool.
- Use the national seller-closing-costs note to read the statement, then match every line to your quotes.
- If you sell without an agent, have a Texas lawyer advise you on the contract form.
- Read the option period and the closing date on any offer before you accept.
- Confirm wires by phone and deliver possession on the contract date.

## Worked examples

### A title quote next to a commission quote

Suppose a title company estimates $3,100 for the owner's policy and settlement services on your price, and a broker quotes $11,000 for the listing side. A buyer asks you to pay $9,000 toward their agent's fee. Those three figures are examples you replace with your quotes. Put them in the net calculator with the payoff. Do not also subtract "about six percent" on top. That would count the broker twice and invent a rate. [Seller closing costs: what sellers pay](/tips/seller-closing-costs) is the glossary for the lines. The Texas estimate is the dollar amount.

If the contract says the seller pays the owner's policy, the $3,100 stays in your column. If the contract says the buyer pays it, it leaves your column. Read that line twice. Custom will not correct a signed contract.

### Selling without a realtor

Suppose you will not hire a listing broker. You still complete the disclosure if the law requires it for this house, you still need a contract a title company can close, and you still need showing hours you can keep. A typical range for a Texas lawyer to review one offer is something you ask that lawyer to quote. A few hundred dollars is a common conversation, and a contested estate is not that conversation. Get the fee in writing. Budget it beside the title estimate. The national FSBO notes cover hours and buyer questions. They do not replace the TREC form or the lawyer.

## Common mistakes

- Checking "no" on the disclosure because you have not looked in the attic.
- Using a contract from another state.
- Subtracting a guessed percent and a real commission quote.
- Ignoring the option period until a buyer terminates.
- Letting a title company "whoever the buyer uses" send wire instructions you never confirm.
- Promising possession the weekend before the contract date because the buyer is ready.

## When to call a pro

Call a Texas real estate lawyer if you are selling without a broker, if the house is in an estate or a trust, if the disclosure question does not match your facts, or if a buyer claims you hid a defect. Call the title company for the fee estimate and the wire phone number you will save in your contacts. Call a broker if you want the job list handled and you have read the listing agreement's term and pay. Call the loan servicer for the payoff. The TREC website is the form library. It is not your lawyer.

You can walk the house, gather the old repair bills, and request the quotes yourself. Do that before the first showing. The lawyer and the title company are how the form and the money stay valid.

## Questions

### Do I have to use a realtor in Texas?

No. Many sellers hire one. If you do not, you still have the disclosure and the contract to get right. The note on selling without a realtor walks that path. TREC requires license holders to use promulgated forms. You, if you are unlicensed, should ask a lawyer which form to sign rather than improvising.

### What are seller closing costs in Texas?

The categories match the national closing-costs note: payoff, broker compensation you agreed to, title and escrow charges allocated in the contract, prorations, and credits. The amounts are your title quote and your contract. There is no single Texas percent on this site.

### How long is the option period?

It is the number of days in the offer you sign, not a statewide constant. Read it. During that period a buyer may have a termination right they paid for. Ask your lawyer or broker what the paragraph in your form allows before you spend the option fee money in your head.

### Does the disclosure cover a house in a trust or an estate?

Ask a Texas lawyer. Estates and trusts have their own authority problems, covered in the inherited-house guide, and the disclosure may still be required. Do not skip the notice only because the owner died. Confirm it.
