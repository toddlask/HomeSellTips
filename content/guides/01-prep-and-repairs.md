Prep is a sequence, not a shopping trip. Water, heat, power, and a door that opens come before paint and a deep clean. A finish coat on a wall that still leaks only makes a second mess. This guide walks that order. The short notes stay on their own pages, including the [Pre-listing walkthrough](/tips/walk-the-house-before-you-hire) and [What to fix before selling a house](/tips/fix-function-before-finish).

## Write the list before you hire

Start outside and write what you can point at. Roof edge, walk, and door are three lines, not one job called the front. Inside, run each faucet, flip each switch, and open each window that should open. One line names the room and the fault. "Hall bath, tub faucet drips" is useful. "Bathroom needs work" is not.

Sort every line into fix, disclose, or leave. Fix means the house needs it to work, or the fault will show in a photo. Disclose means you will say it in writing even if you do not repair it. Leave means you decided not to spend, and you will not pretend the work was done. The [Selling a house as is vs fixing up: how to decide](/tips/selling-as-is-vs-fixing-up) page is the short version of that sort. This guide is the order of the work you keep.

## Function before finish

Do the jobs that stop damage first. A drip, a soft floor, a gutter that dumps water at the foundation, and a stain you have not traced all sit ahead of cushions and paint. [Water stain on ceiling selling house](/tips/trace-a-stain-to-its-source) is the short note on following a stain to the source before you cover it. [Clean gutters before selling house](/tips/clear-gutters-before-showings) is the short note on the water path at the roof edge.

Then make the house operate. [Smoke detector requirements when selling a house](/tips/test-alarms-before-you-list) covers a test of the alarms. [HVAC check before selling house](/tips/run-heat-and-cooling-before-showings) covers running heat and cooling before someone walks through. [Small repairs before selling house](/tips/tighten-loose-hardware) covers loose hinges, pulls, and latches. None of those notes is a bid. They are the checks you do so a bid has a real subject.

Finish work comes after the dust. [Recaulk before selling house](/tips/replace-cracked-caulk-before-photos) belongs at the end of the wet jobs, not the morning of the photo shoot. [Deep cleaning before selling house](/tips/deep-clean-after-the-dusty-work) belongs after the last dirty trade leaves. Cleaning first, then sanding, only means you clean twice.

## A week of work, in order

Day one is the list and the sort. Day two is calls for anything you marked fix and cannot price: a plumber for an active leak, an electrician for a dead circuit, a roofer if you can see a failed flashing from the ground. Ask each person for a written bid that says what is included and what is not. A spoken range in a doorway is not a number you can subtract from a price.

Days three and four are the function jobs you can do or that a hired person finishes: hardware, alarms, filters, gutters if the height is safe for you, and the stain you already traced. Days five and six are finish: caulk, paint touch-up, and the clean. If a trade is still cutting, the clean waits. The house does not need to look new. It needs to match the list you will hand a buyer.

## Checklist

- Walk once in daylight and once with the lights on, and write one line per fault.
- Mark each line fix, disclose, or leave.
- Trace any stain to a source before you paint over it.
- Stop active water before you book a cleaner.
- Test alarms, heat, and cooling, and write the result.
- Tighten hardware and note any door that still sticks.
- Replace cracked caulk after the wet work, not before it.
- Clean after the dusty work is finished.
- Put every written bid into the net proceeds calculator before you pick a list price.
- Keep the disclose lines for the form your state uses. Do not bury them in a note about paint.

## Worked examples

### A drip, a hinge, and a cleaner

Suppose the list has three lines: a hall-bath supply line that weeps, a bedroom door that scrapes, and dusty floors after a drywall patch. A typical range for a plumber to replace a short supply line on an open, easy run is about $150 to $400. A typical range for a handyman hour to plane or adjust one door is about $75 to $200. A typical range for a one-time house clean after the dust settles is about $200 to $500, and more if the house is large or the clean includes inside appliances. Those are labeled typical ranges, not a bid for your house. You add the written numbers, not these ranges, in the [net proceeds calculator](/tools/net-proceeds).

If you paint the bath before the supply line is dry, you pay for the paint twice. If you clean before the drywall patch is sanded, you pay for the clean twice. The order is the savings. The ranges only tell you the size of each line so you can decide which ones to buy.

### Leaving a job and saying so

Suppose a window in a spare room fogs between the panes. A typical range to replace one insulated glass unit varies widely with the size and the frame, so do not use a national guess. Get one written price. If you leave it, the line moves from fix to disclose. You do not caulk the frame and hope a buyer calls the fog a reflection. The walkthrough note says the same thing in fewer words: fix, disclose, or leave, and write which one you chose.

## Common mistakes

- Painting over a stain you have not traced.
- Hiring a cleaner while a trade is still sanding or cutting.
- Bundling three small jobs into one vague "make it nice" bid.
- Testing the heat on the morning of the first visit and discovering it does not run.
- Calling a repair finished because the part is in the garage, not because it is installed.
- Dropping a disclose item because the room looks better with the light off.

## When to call a pro

Call a licensed plumber for an active leak, a sewer smell, or a drain you have already snaked once. Call a licensed electrician for a warm outlet, a breaker that trips again, or any wire you cannot identify. Call a roofer if the leak path is above the ceiling and you cannot see the source from a safe place on the ground. Do not climb a steep roof to prove a point.

Call a structural person, not a painter, if a floor feels soft underfoot or a door frame has newly pulled away from the wall. Foundation movement has its own guide on this site. For ordinary prep, the test is simple: if the repair could hurt someone, start a fire, or hide damage, it is not a weekend project. Get the written bid, put that figure in your net sheet, and decide fix or disclose with the number in front of you.

## Questions

### Do I have to remodel before I list?

No. This site does not set a repair standard. Buyers and inspectors will still look at function. A house that leaks, smells of sewer, or has dead alarms is a different sale from a house with scuffed paint. Sort the list. Spend on function first. Leave the rest in writing.

### Should I fix everything an inspector might mention?

No. You cannot guess every note. Fix what you already know is broken, disclose what you know and will not fix, and leave cosmetic wear that you have decided not to buy. [Repair requests after inspection](/tips/ask-who-pays-for-requested-repairs) is about the later conversation, after a buyer has a report.

### What if two bids are far apart?

Ask each bidder to list what is included. A low number that excludes the patch, the paint, or the haul-away is not the low number. Compare the same scope. Then type the one you accept into the proceeds tool before you publish a price.

### Can I do the clean myself?

Yes. The point is timing. Clean after the dusty work. A self-clean costs your hours instead of a service invoice. Write those hours down if you are also deciding whether to hire an agent. Time is part of the job list, even when the invoice is zero.
