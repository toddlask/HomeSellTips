Listing photos are a walk the camera takes once. The order, the light, and the rooms you have already prepped matter more than a filter. Shoot after the repairs and the staging, not before. The short notes are [Best time of day for real estate photos](/tips/daylight-and-lights), [Listing photo order](/tips/shoot-in-the-order-a-visitor-walks), and [Keep the camera level and skip the distortion](/tips/keep-the-camera-level).

## Before the camera comes out

Finish the prep that would show up as a stain, a trash can, or a car in the driveway. [Prepare house for real estate photos](/tips/close-lids-and-hide-trash-cans) and [Exterior listing photos tips](/tips/move-cars-before-the-photo-appointment) are the short checks. [Listing photos of upgrades](/tips/show-the-repairs-you-finished) is how you show work you actually completed, not work you meant to schedule. If the repair is not done, do not shoot a corner that pretends it is.

Turn on the lights you want in the photo and open the coverings the way you will for showings. Mixed light is a choice you make on purpose. A lamp that is off in the only wide shot of the living room is a dark room, even at noon. [Best time of day for real estate photos](/tips/daylight-and-lights) covers daylight plus the lights, not daylight instead of the lights.

## The walk, then the details

Shoot in the order a visitor walks: curb, entry, main room, kitchen, bedrooms, baths, then yard and utility spaces you are willing to show. [Listing photo order](/tips/shoot-in-the-order-a-visitor-walks) is that path. For each room, take one wide frame and one detail. [How many listing photos](/tips/take-one-wide-shot-and-one-detail) is the short rule. Twenty blurry frames are not better than eight level ones.

Hold the camera level. A tilted fridge and a leaning door are the fastest way to make a straight house look wrong. [Keep the camera level and skip the distortion](/tips/keep-the-camera-level) is the short note. Step back before you reach for a wide setting that bows the walls. If you cannot get the whole room without bending the lines, shoot two frames.

Delete the bad frames the same day, while you can still reshoot. [Editing listing photos](/tips/delete-blurry-frames-the-same-day) is that pass. Do not hand a photographer's card, or your own, a set you have not looked at.

## What the photos promise

Every frame is a statement. If the counter is clear in the photo, clear it again for the showing. If the cars were moved, do not leave a truck on the walk for the first visit. The staging guide is the reset. The photo guide is the record. They have to match.

Skip rooms you will not show, and do not hide a fault with a crop if you already decided to disclose it. A photo that avoids a water stain does not erase the stain. It only makes the first visit feel like a trick. Shoot the house you are selling. If a room is unfinished, leave it out of the set or show it and disclose it. A missing photo is easier to explain than a photo that edits the house.

## Checklist

- Finish repairs and the staging path before the shoot.
- Move cars off the driveway and the walk you want in the frame.
- Close lids, hide trash cans, and clear the counters you will show.
- Turn on the lights and set the window coverings to match the showings.
- Shoot curb, entry, then the interior in walking order.
- Take one wide frame and one detail in each room you include.
- Keep the camera level and reshoot any frame that bows a wall.
- Include the repairs you finished, and leave unfinished work out or disclose it.
- Review and delete blurry frames the same day.
- Save the set you will upload, and do not restage the house out from under it.

## Worked examples

### A morning with a phone

Suppose you will shoot the house yourself on a bright morning. You need a charged phone, a cloth for the lens, and about two hours including the reset. A typical range for a small tripod, if you want the level line to stay put, is about $20 to $60. A typical range for a replacement bulb pack so the dark fixture is not in the wide shot is about $10 to $30. There is no invoice for your time. Write the two hours on the same list you use when you compare hiring an agent. If the first wide shot of the kitchen is soft, delete it before you leave the room. Reshooting tomorrow means resetting the counters tomorrow.

### Hiring the hour

Suppose a local photographer quotes a set number of edited frames for a house of your size. A typical range for a standard residential photo appointment is about $150 to $500, and twilight, drone, or video adds a separate line. Ask what is included: the number of final frames, the turnaround, and whether you may reshoot one room if a trade is still in it. A low quote that delivers unedited bursts is not the same product as a small edited set. Pay for the set you will actually upload. Do not schedule the shoot on the same morning as a plumber. The short prep notes exist so the house is quiet and finished when the camera arrives.

## Common mistakes

- Shooting before the repair or the deep clean.
- Leaving cars and bins in the curb photo.
- Using a wide setting that bends the cabinets.
- Uploading every frame, including the blurry ones.
- Photographing a styled corner that the showing will not repeat.
- Cropping out a fault you are required, or have decided, to disclose.

## When to call a pro

Hire a photographer when the rooms are ready and you want a consistent level and a consistent edit. Hire them after, not before, the function repairs. If the roof line or a tall stair is the subject and you would be standing somewhere unsafe, that is a photographer or a drone operator with permission, not a chair you drag onto a slope.

You can shoot a clear, level set yourself if the house is bright and the path is ready. The test is the same-day review. If you cannot tell which frames are straight, hire the hour. Ask for the price in writing. A verbal "a few hundred" is not a scope.

## Questions

### How many photos do I need?

Enough to walk the house: outside, each main room, and the spaces you are proud of or willing to show. One wide and one detail per room is a floor, not a ceiling. Fifty near-duplicates of the sofa do not help. [How many listing photos](/tips/take-one-wide-shot-and-one-detail) is the short version.

### Should I wait for a sunny day?

Wait for enough daylight to see corners, and turn the lights on anyway. A dark storm day makes glass and lamps do more of the work. Do not wait a month for a perfect sky if the house is ready and the list date is real.

### Do phone photos work?

Yes, if they are level, in focus, and shot in order. A phone does not fix a cluttered counter or a car in the frame. Prep still comes first. Delete the misses the same day.

### Can I edit out a crack or a stain?

Do not edit out a condition you would have to disclose or that a buyer will see in person. Straightening a tilt and lifting a dark corner is different from erasing a fault. If you are unsure, leave the frame honest and put the fault on the disclosure.
