The choice is who does each job, not a slogan. List the work, price the hours, and name the person who answers buyers, holds the forms, and schedules the showings. Then hire or keep the list. The short notes are [Realtor vs FSBO](/tips/list-the-jobs-before-you-choose), [Is FSBO worth it](/tips/price-the-hours-you-will-spend), and [Realtor commission changes: what sellers need to know](/tips/realtor-commission-changes).

## The jobs, written as names

Write every job on one page: pricing notes, photos, advertising, showings, questions from buyers, forms, disclosure, negotiation, and the closing file. Put a name next to each line. The name is you, an agent, an attorney, a photographer, or a title company. A blank line is a job that will land on you at the worst time. [Realtor vs FSBO](/tips/list-the-jobs-before-you-choose) is that page in short form.

If you sell it yourself, you still need answers for strangers. [Selling a house by owner](/tips/questions-if-you-sell-it-yourself) and [FSBO buyer questions](/tips/decide-who-answers-buyer-questions) cover who speaks for the house. [FSBO paperwork](/tips/learn-which-forms-you-must-use) is the prompt to learn which forms your state expects. This site cannot give you the form. A Texas seller has a separate guide that points at the Texas Real Estate Commission. In any state, ask a local attorney or a broker which contract a seller in your shoes is supposed to use.

## Hours and money are the same decision

Price the hours you will spend on calls, showings, and paperwork before you decide the commission quote is too high. [Is FSBO worth it](/tips/price-the-hours-you-will-spend) is that comparison. An hour is not free because no invoice arrived. If you cannot leave work for a midday showing, the hour has a cost even when you love the idea of selling it yourself.

Compensation is negotiated. The [National Association of Realtors settlement facts](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/what-the-nar-settlement-means-for-home-buyers-and-sellers) explain what changed about offers of compensation. This page does not set a rate. Put the quotes you received into the [commission comparison](/tools/commission): full service, a sale where you pay a buyer's agent and not a listing side, and a sale where you pay neither. The tool uses your numbers. [Realtor commission changes: what sellers need to know](/tips/realtor-commission-changes) is the short reading beside that tool.

## What you will not negotiate, and the hours you will keep

Write the terms you will not trade away: a possession date, a fixture you are taking, a price under the bottom line. [Seller negotiation tips](/tips/write-what-you-will-not-negotiate) belongs to the offers stage and starts here, because the list should exist before a buyer asks. Write showing hours you can keep. [FSBO showings](/tips/plan-showing-hours-before-you-advertise) is the short version. Advertising a house you cannot open is how the first week goes quiet.

If you hire an agent, the same lists still exist. You are paying someone to hold some of the lines. Read the listing agreement for the term, the pay, and what happens if you cancel. Ask who answers the phone and who presents offers. Those are job names, not compliments.

## Checklist

- List every job from pricing through closing and put a name on each line.
- Decide who answers buyer questions and how fast.
- Learn which contract and disclosure forms your state expects, from an attorney or a broker.
- Price your hours for showings, calls, and paperwork.
- Collect written quotes for listing pay and for any offer to a buyer's agent.
- Run those quotes through the commission comparison before you sign.
- Write showing hours you can keep.
- Write the terms you will not negotiate.
- Read the listing agreement's term and cancellation lines before you sign it.
- Keep the job list in the same folder as the pricing sheet.

## Worked examples

### Three ways to pay for help

Suppose you might sell at $400,000. You have three written quotes. A listing agreement quotes $12,000 to the listing side. A buyer-agent conversation assumes you might offer $10,000. A FSBO plan assumes you pay neither, and you hire an attorney to review the contract for a flat fee. A typical range for a flat contract review varies by city and by how many drafts you ask for. Get the fee in writing. Do not invent it. Put $12,000, $10,000, and $0 into the commission tool as the cases you were actually quoted, and add the attorney's written fee to the FSBO case by hand. The tool will not know about that invoice unless you treat it as part of your cost.

The hours sit beside the dollars. If you expect 30 showings and 20 hours of calls, write that. If the listing quote includes those hours, you are comparing a check to a calendar, not a check to zero.

### A job you forgot

Suppose you assigned photos, pricing, and showings, and left disclosure blank. In many states the seller still has to complete a disclosure of known material facts. The person who "is handling it" needs a name. If the name is you, block an evening to read the form with your notes from the prep walk. If the name is your agent, ask when they need it back. A blank disclosure line is how a known leak stays off the form.

## Common mistakes

- Treating FSBO as free because there is no commission invoice.
- Signing a listing term you have not read, including what happens if you cancel.
- Advertising before anyone owns the showings and the forms.
- Assuming a buyer's agent will appear without a conversation about pay.
- Using a contract from another state because it was free online.
- Skipping the disclosure because the house "has nothing wrong." Known facts still get written down.

## When to call a pro

Call a real estate attorney when you are selling without a broker and you need the contract, the disclosure duties, and a review of an offer. Ask for the fee and what it includes. Call two agents for written interviews if you want a quote and a job list, even if you later sell it yourself. You are buying information. [Realtor vs FSBO](/tips/list-the-jobs-before-you-choose) is the question list to take into those meetings.

Call a title company early to learn who holds the earnest money and which fees a seller pays in your county. Do not wait until a buyer names a company you have never heard of and asks you to wire a deposit the same day. The closing guide covers the wire call. The decision to hire starts with the job names.

## Questions

### Is one choice always cheaper?

No. A commission quote can be larger than your time is worth, or smaller than the mistakes a missed form can cause. Run your quotes and your hours. This site does not rank the choice.

### Can I hire an agent only for the paperwork?

Sometimes. Ask a broker whether they offer a limited-service agreement and what they will not do. Get it in writing. A handshake that "they will look at the offer" is not a plan when a deadline is tomorrow.

### What did the NAR settlement change?

Read the association's own page, linked above. The practical point for a seller is that compensation is negotiated and should be written in your agreements, not assumed from an old rate card. Use the numbers you were quoted.

### Do I still need a disclosure if I sell it myself?

In many states, yes. The duty follows the seller, not the sign in the yard. Ask a local attorney which form applies. Do not copy a packet from a friend in another state.
